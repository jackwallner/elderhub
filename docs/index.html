The Pitch

Most medication apps assume you are tracking your own pills. Elderhub assumes you are not.

If you help look after a parent or a spouse, the real list lives in three places at once: some of it in your head, some on a scrap of paper in a kitchen drawer, and some in whatever the pharmacy printed out last spring. Then a nurse asks what she takes, and you are guessing. Elderhub keeps one current list per person, so you always have the answer, and so does your sister.

What Mattered

Offline first is the product, not an optimisation. Every screen renders from the copy on the phone. Sync writes into that copy and never sits in front of it, because the scenario the app exists for is an emergency room with no bars.

A missing section is an answer. The emergency card and the exported one-pager print every critical heading even when it is empty, as "ALLERGIES: not recorded". A section that simply vanishes reads as "no allergies" to whoever is holding the page.

Agreement is not a conflict. Two siblings ticking off the same errand is the same intent twice, so the shared task list merges it. Two people editing the same task's wording is a real conflict, and that is handled separately.

The check-in claims nothing. It reports that a button was not pressed. It cannot tell whether anyone is unwell, it does not detect falls, and it never calls for help. Nothing in the app is allowed to imply otherwise, in the app or in a notification.

Not a medical device

Elderhub is an organisational tool. It does not diagnose, treat or cure any condition, and it does not provide medical advice. Always follow the guidance of a licensed clinician. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
