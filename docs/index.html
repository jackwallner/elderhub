How it is built and why it works that way.

The Pitch

Most medication apps assume you are tracking your own pills. Elderhub assumes you are not.

When a family looks after someone, medications, appointments, readings, tasks, bills, notes and contacts end up spread across a phone, a calendar, a text thread and a kitchen drawer. Elderhub keeps one current care record per person, so the next person helping has the same answer.

Elderhub Plus

Every feature is free for one care recipient, including medications, visits, readings, tasks, notes, bills, check-in, emergency card and export. Plus adds additional care recipients to the same care circle.

One person pays. The purchase covers the whole care circle. Family members who join are never charged as separate seats.

Prices. Monthly, yearly, or a one-time lifetime purchase are available. Eligible new subscribers get a 1-week free trial on subscriptions. Prices are shown in the app before you buy and vary by region. Subscriptions renew automatically unless cancelled at least 24 hours before the period ends, and are managed in your Apple ID settings.

What Mattered

Offline first is the product, not an optimisation. Every screen renders from the copy on the phone. Sync writes into that copy and never sits in front of it, because the scenario the app exists for is an emergency room with no bars.

A missing section is an answer. The emergency card and the exported one-pager print every critical heading even when it is empty, as "ALLERGIES: not recorded". A section that simply vanishes reads as "no allergies" to whoever is holding the page.

Shared does not mean everything is exposed. A family can work in one care circle while access follows the people whose records are being kept. The app keeps those choices in its access rules, not just in the buttons on screen.

The check-in claims nothing. It reports that a button was not pressed. It cannot tell whether anyone is unwell, it does not detect falls, and it never calls for help. Nothing in the app is allowed to imply otherwise, in the app or in a notification.

Not a medical device

Elderhub is an organizational tool. It does not diagnose, treat or cure any condition, and it does not provide medical advice. Always follow the guidance of a licensed clinician. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
