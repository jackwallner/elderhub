Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 17, 2026

This policy is provided separately from the general privacy policy for people in Washington State, under the My Health My Data Act, and for people in Nevada under SB 370. It describes consumer health data specifically. Everything here is also true of the general policy; nothing in one contradicts the other.

Much of what Elderhub holds is consumer health data, and much of it is about someone other than the person who typed it. The app also holds care coordination and financial information that may be connected to that health data. That is the point of the app, and it is why this page exists.

Categories of Consumer Health Data Collected

  • Health conditions and status: conditions, allergies, blood type, and free-text notes about a person's health.
  • Medications and treatment: drug names, strengths, forms, what each medication is for, the prescribing doctor, the dispensing pharmacy, dosing schedules, and quantities on hand.
  • Use of health care services: doctor visits, the reason for each, notes from them, follow-ups, and scheduled appointments.
  • Measurements: vital signs typed in by a person, such as blood pressure, weight, temperature or blood sugar.
  • Health-related events: falls, emergency room trips, symptoms and similar incidents recorded in the care log.
  • Bodily functions and daily activity: whether a scheduled dose was taken or skipped, and the time a person pressed their daily check-in button.
  • Care coordination and family notes: tasks, assignees, completion history, free-form notes, and bills recorded for a person's care. Bills may also be financial information. Elderhub does not store bank credentials or connect to a bank.
  • Identifiers connected to the above: the name, relationship and date of birth of each person tracked, provider details, and emergency contact names and phone numbers.

Elderhub does not collect precise location data. The app never requests location permission and contains no location code. It does not collect biometric identifiers, does not read from Apple Health, and does not connect to any monitor, wearable or medical device. Every value in the app was typed in by a person.

Sources of Consumer Health Data

One source only: what you and the other members of your care circle type into the app. Elderhub does not buy, license, receive or infer health data from any other party, and does not obtain it from data brokers, health care providers, pharmacies, insurers or advertising networks.

Purposes for Collecting and Using It

  • To show you the medication list, emergency card, visit history, care events, tasks, notes, bills, and other records you entered.
  • To sync that record between the phones of the people in your family group, so everyone looking after someone sees the same list.
  • To send the local reminders and, if the daily check-in is turned on, to tell caregivers when a check-in did not happen in the agreed window.
  • To keep the record available with no signal, which requires a full copy on the device.
  • To let you choose whether to share an emergency-card copy through the iOS share sheet. That copy is sent to the app or person you choose, not automatically to Elderhub.

Consumer health data is never used for advertising, for profiling, for training any model, for any form of behavioral analytics, or for any purpose beyond running the features you turned on.

Categories of Data Shared, and With Whom

Elderhub does not sell consumer health data, and has never done so. Selling it, in the sense the My Health My Data Act uses, would require your separate written authorization; there is no circumstance in which it is offered for sale, so no such authorization is ever sought.

Elderhub shares health data as part of operating the service in two ways:

  • With the other members of your own care circle. This is the feature. Who can see what is set by each member's role, enforced by the database itself, and shown in full to a person before anything of theirs syncs. Organizers and caregivers can see the shared care records; a person in the being-supported role can see only permitted parts of their own record. Family notes and bills are staff-only.
  • With one processor, Supabase Inc., which hosts the encrypted database and the sync service on our behalf, under contract, and may not use the data for its own purposes. The shared database uses row-level access controls, but free-text notes and bill notes are not client-side or end-to-end encrypted. See Supabase security.

Two other services are used for limited functions. Apple provides Sign in with Apple, in-app purchases, and push notification delivery. A missed check-in push contains the recipient's name and a factual no-check-in message, not medication or diagnosis details. RevenueCat receives the purchase receipt and linked app account identifier for subscription checks, not the care record. No advertising network, data broker, analytics vendor, or affiliate receives the care record, because there are none.

Storage and Retention

A record can remain local to one phone without an account or care circle. When you explicitly share it, the app keeps a local offline copy and syncs a shared copy to Supabase. The local store uses iOS file protection with NSFileProtectionCompleteUntilFirstUserAuthentication; it is not end-to-end encrypted. Deleted synced rows may remain as server tombstones while the care circle exists, and an audit log can retain old and new snapshots for medications, doses, people, and membership changes.

Your Rights

You may, at any time and at no cost:

  • Confirm whether we hold your consumer health data, and access it. Everything is visible in the app. The emergency card exports as plain text from inside it.
  • Learn the list of third parties it has been shared with. That list is the section above, and it is complete.
  • Withdraw your consent to its collection and sharing. Leaving your care circle stops that account's access and removes the shared copy from that phone. Deleting your account removes the account from the service, but does not erase the shared record kept for the rest of the family.
  • Have it deleted. Delete individual entries in the app, or your whole account under Settings → Delete my account. Individual deletion is hidden in the app and follows the sync and audit retention rules above. Deleting your account deliberately does not erase the family's shared care record, which belongs to everyone looking after that person. To request deletion of the shared record, email the address below and it will be actioned within 30 days.

To exercise any of these by email, write to jackwallner+medlist@gmail.com. A request is verified by confirming control of the email address or Apple relay address on the account. There is no fee, and exercising a right will never result in a worse experience in the app.

If a request is refused, the reason will be given in the reply, and you may appeal by replying to it. If an appeal is denied you may complain to the Washington State Attorney General.

Data About Other People

Most of what is in Elderhub is health data about someone other than the person who entered it. When you add a person who will not be using the app themselves, Elderhub asks you to confirm that they know you keep this record or that you are the person who makes their health decisions, and records that confirmation. Please enter someone else's health information only if that is true. A person who later joins with their own account is shown exactly who is in the group and what each member can see about them, before anything of theirs syncs, and may leave at any time.

Changes

Material changes to this policy will be posted here with a revised date before they take effect, and consumer health data already collected will not be used for a new purpose without consent.
