What Elderhub Is, and Is Not

Elderhub helps a family write down and share medications, doses, allergies, conditions, doctor visits and emergency contacts for the people they look after.

Elderhub is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent anything, and it does not give medical advice. Nothing in it replaces a licensed clinician.

Elderhub is not an emergency service. The daily check-in tells the caregivers in a family group that a button was not pressed. It cannot detect a fall, an illness or an emergency, it does not summon help, and it must not be relied on as a safety system. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

Reminders and notifications depend on your device, your notification settings and your network. They can be late or fail to arrive. Do not use Elderhub as the only way anyone takes a medication.

Accuracy

Everything in the app was typed in by a person. You are responsible for what you enter and for keeping it current. Elderhub does not verify drug names, dosages or interactions.

Accounts & Care Circles

Elderhub uses Sign in with Apple. You are responsible for activity on your account.

An account belongs to one care circle, which may contain multiple people such as Mom, Dad, or a partner. Members with the organizer or caregiver role can see and edit every person in the circle. A member in the “being supported” role sees only their own record.

Only add another person's health information to a family group if they know you are keeping it, or if you are the person who makes their health decisions. Anyone in the “being supported” role may leave the group at any time, and the group is told when they do.
